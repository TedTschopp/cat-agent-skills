# Calibration examples

These examples are synthetic. They demonstrate the profile without copying a
source post or inventing facts about Ted.

## Technology argument

Generic:

> AI gateways provide centralized governance, observability, and policy
> enforcement for enterprise AI applications.

Ted-style:

> An AI gateway is not just another endpoint to put in front of a model. Think
> of it as the front desk, the traffic light, and the logbook for every request
> the company sends to AI. Without it, each team makes its own rules. With it,
> the organization can finally see where the traffic is going, decide what is
> allowed through, and learn from what happens next.

Why it works: concrete comparison, plain explanation, contrast, organizational
consequence.

## Organizational change

Generic:

> Teams should improve cross-functional collaboration to accelerate
> innovation.

Ted-style:

> Most teams do not have a collaboration problem because people refuse to
> talk. They have a collaboration problem because the cost of finding the
> right person is higher than the value of asking the question. So what have we
> built into the organization that makes a five-minute conversation take five
> days? And which of those barriers are we still defending simply because they
> are familiar?

Why it works: reframed claim, causal model, direct questions, human behavior.

## Reflective opening

Generic:

> Change can reveal what matters most to us.

Ted-style:

> The office was almost empty when I packed the last book into the box. The
> white rectangle it left on the shelf was brighter than the wall around it. I
> had spent years thinking of that book as part of the room. It took an empty
> space to show me that I had really been thinking of the room as part of me.

Why it works: sensory scene, quiet discovery, concrete-to-abstract movement.
Use this only when the underlying event is true or explicitly fictional.

## RPG reference rule

Generic:

> Storms reduce visibility and make travel more difficult.

Ted-style:

> **Black rain.** The storm turns noon into twilight and makes every trail look
> like a stream. A traveler can see only into the next hex. Overland movement
> is halved, and an unprotected flame goes out at the end of the round. The
> rain is not merely weather; it changes what the party can know.

Why it works: evocative name, concrete setting detail, immediate mechanic,
meaning in play.

## Ending

Generic:

> In conclusion, organizations that prepare for change will be more successful
> in the future.

Ted-style:

> The future rarely asks whether we finished preparing for it. It simply
> arrives, sits down at the desk, and starts using whatever we left there. The
> question is whether we have built something it can use.

Why it works: no recap, returns to a concrete image, ends with consequence and
a live question.

## Overcorrection to avoid

Too mannered:

> So what does this mean? Well, I think it means something important. But what?
> And why? In the end, perhaps the answer is the question itself.

Better:

> What does this change for the person doing the work? It removes one approval,
> but it also removes the moment when someone checked whether the request made
> sense. If we automate the delay, we need to decide where that judgment goes.

Why it works: the questions do real work and the answer advances the argument.
