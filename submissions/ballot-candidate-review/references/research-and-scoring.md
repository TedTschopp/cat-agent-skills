# Research and Scoring

## Evidence

Verify ballot identity, date, district, candidate spelling, vote limits, and current candidacy with the official election authority. Separate eligibility from competence. Keep withdrawn, disputed, unverified, and ineligible printed names visible with their status. Ask for a clearer image when an item cannot be read.

Use current statutes, charters, official office descriptions, and election guides for duties. Use audits, budgets, court or disciplinary records, and documented outcomes for performance and conduct. Credible independent reporting adds context and tests contested claims. Candidate materials establish claims or positions; label them. Advocacy arguments and endorsements are interested perspectives, not independent proof.

Apply the same research questions to everyone: background, results, job competence, integrity records, and positions relevant to stated priorities. Coverage can differ because evidence differs, but effort must not favor prominent candidates. Verify identities before attributing records. Do not make a misconduct finding from a matching name alone.

Attach direct source links, publisher, publication date when known, and access date to ratings, positions, and material findings. Distinguish allegations, pending proceedings, and final findings. Include material responses and counterevidence. “No verified finding located in records checked” describes a search, not proof of a clean record. Several syndicated copies are not independent corroboration.

Resolve material conflicts with primary evidence when possible. Otherwise explain the conflict and lower confidence or leave the criterion unrated. Stop when the criteria and priorities have comparable evidence, counterevidence has been considered, and gaps are explicit. Do not fill gaps from party or identity. Show the research cutoff; check appeals, corrections, and changes to older records. Broken or inaccessible sources are gaps.

## Qualification Anchors

Define criterion-specific 0, 2, and 4 anchors before scoring, using these general meanings:

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | Documented serious deficiency or clearly inadequate fit |
| 1 | Limited demonstrated capability relative to the office's demands |
| 2 | Adequate demonstrated capability at a relevant level |
| 3 | Strong demonstrated capability supported by relevant results |
| 4 | Exceptional demonstrated capability supported by substantial relevant evidence |
| null | Insufficient, conflicting, or unavailable evidence |

Zero requires evidence. A researched lack of relevant experience differs from a lack of public information. Transferable experience counts; tenure is not proof of results. Explain judgments without implying objective certification.

For weight `w` and rating `r`, points are `w × r / 4`. Weights total 100; do not round components before summing.

## Missing Evidence and Supported Tiers

Do not redistribute an individual's missing weight across observed criteria:

- Lower bound: sum of observed weighted points.
- Upper bound: lower bound plus all unrated weight.
- Coverage: total rated weight as a percentage of the rubric.
- Show a point score only with complete coverage; otherwise show a range.

These are rubric-based evidence bounds, not statistical confidence intervals. All missing means range 0–100 and 0% coverage: **Insufficient evidence**, not zero and not a leading candidate.

The script excludes candidates from supported ordering when eligibility is unconfirmed or ineligible, or qualification coverage is below 70%. This is a disclosed report threshold, not a legal or professional standard. An unnamed write-in has uncertain eligibility and missing ratings until identified.

A is supported above B only if A's lower bound exceeds B's upper bound. The script builds successive tiers from this relation. Overlapping bounds can share a provisional tier; names inside a tier appear alphabetically, not as a hidden tiebreaker. A shared tier does not claim equal merit. Explain close calls and the evidence or reasonable weight changes that could alter them.

## Policy Alignment

Request desired outcomes and positive relative weights for this election. Normalize them to 100 and show the result. A topic alone is not a position. Dealbreakers must be explicit, scoped, and evidence-supported. Do not deduct qualification points for disagreement.

| Rating | Relation to the Voter's Stated Outcome |
|---|---|
| 0 | Documented strong opposition |
| 1 | Documented position mostly conflicts |
| 2 | Documented mixed, conditional, or neutral position |
| 3 | Documented position mostly aligns |
| 4 | Documented strong alignment |
| null | No sufficiently clear position or record |

Use the same arithmetic and evidence bounds. Missing is not neutral. Distinguish a promise, record, feasibility, and authority. Alignment is not an endorsement of a platform's truth or wisdom. Keep the two assessments separate.

If a priority genuinely cannot apply to an item, exclude it for every candidate or both measure options before scoring, explain the contest-level exclusion, and normalize the remainder. Never exclude a priority merely because one candidate's position is missing. With no applicable priorities, alignment is unassessed. Evaluate a measure's Yes and No options independently; neither is automatically the other's inverse. Retention compares an incumbent's record to the standard and explains actual Yes/No effects.

## Confidence

Use High, Moderate, or Low with a reason tied to source quality, coverage, freshness, and contradictions. Confidence is not a probability and is not automatically coverage. A campaign biography alone cannot establish high confidence in performance or integrity.

Use “strongest supported qualifications under this rubric,” “alignment with your stated priorities,” “close call,” “provisional tier,” and “insufficient evidence” when appropriate. Do not claim an objectively best candidate or hide uncertainty behind precise totals.
